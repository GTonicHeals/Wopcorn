Wopcorn · Design direction · v1

House lights down.
The posters do the talking.

Wopcorn is a private movie tracker for a circle of friends — used at night, on a phone, deciding what to watch. The interface is a dark, warm room: near-chromeless surfaces in roasted browns, so poster art supplies all the color. One gold runs through it, and it means exactly one thing: you — your rating, your lists, your place in the app. Fraunces speaks only when a film is named or a screen begins; Inter does the quiet work. The signature is the queue: the only ordered list in the app, so the only place numerals appear — and position № 1 becomes tonight's answer.

Component decisions

The states most likely to be built wrong, decided here.

Star input

The hit area is a 56px full-width row — drag anywhere along it; glyphs are 32px and only display the value. Slider semantics: arrows step ½ star, Home/End jump. Clearing is its own button, never "tap the same value again."

Poster placeholders

Films without artwork get a duotone drawn from the era of the film, with the title set small in the display face — a missing poster still looks intentional.
